## Boundaries and verification gaps

### Bicuspid TAVI and aorta update — 11 October 2026

The 2025 guideline section 8.5.1.2 and Recommendation Table 4 reserve bicuspid TAVI consideration for severe stenosis with increased surgical risk and suitable anatomy (IIb B). SAVR remains the primary mode, particularly in younger patients, coexistent aortopathy or unfavorable morphology. Heavy cusp calcium and calcified raphe increase root injury, leak and mortality risk; no universal calcium/device-sizing cutoff is encoded.

[ESC PAD/aortic disease 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae179), Recommendation Table 68, was checked against the published full text. It recommends entire thoracic CT/CMR assessment, addresses coarctation, family/heritable risk and growth, recommends aortic surgery at maximum diameter ≥55 mm or root-phenotype aortopathy ≥50 mm, and considers concomitant root/ascending replacement at ≥45 mm when valve surgery is indicated. Ascending-phenotype surgery decisions around 50–52 mm also depend on surgical risk and modifiers; they remain specialist-reviewed, not automatic device eligibility thresholds. Growth ≥3 mm/year is a risk modifier, not inferred from an absent prior scan.

The existing structural report now records valve morphology; bicuspid phenotype, raphe/cusp calcium and landing-zone review; sinus/STJ/ascending diameters; thoracic CT/CMR, coarctation and risk assessment; and the specialist aortic strategy. Incomplete reports may be retained, but scheduling requires actual measurements and complete review. Recorded bicuspid evidence cannot be bypassed with a tricuspid answer. Bicuspid TAVI requires increased surgical risk, a concordant valve/aorta Heart Team decision and a dated aortic surveillance/specialist review task. Known aortic surgical indications block routine isolated TAVI booking. A documented prohibitive-risk exception requires actual specialist rationale in the report conclusion, surgery ineligibility and an explicit matching clinician decision; it does not treat the aneurysm or automatically dismiss residual risk. Pure bicuspid AR remains specialist work-up outside this stenosis branch.

Prior preparation clearance is invalidated by the assessment revision and by new/corrected imaging. Morphology-specific work-up cannot be completed by a tricuspid report. Bicuspid fields are inactive for TEER. No new database table or migration is needed.

Follow-up dates remain clinician chosen. ESC VHD 2025 recommends a 6-month stability check when baseline aorta is >45 mm, then yearly imaging; ESC aorta 2024 discusses 1-year then 2–3-year imaging for stable >40-mm BAV after excluding rapid progression. The pathway asks for a dated individualized surveillance/specialist review instead of imposing one interval on all phenotypes.

Exact current regional instructions for each TAVI, mitral/tricuspid clip and replacement device were not verified individually. The clinician must confirm the actual current IFU and implant-team regimen. No universal TEER DAPT duration, implant-size algorithm, coronary-height/coaptation/neo-LVOT eligibility cutoff or automatic drug transition is encoded. Measurement ranges in forms are reporting-validation bounds, not treatment criteria. Scheduling confirms a documented team decision; it is not an independently validated risk calculator or substitute for specialist review.

The preparation and aftercare reminders are workflow checks against the cited assessment/follow-up requirements. They introduce no local numerical treatment threshold. All content uses the existing decision/plan/study/procedure engine. A corrected or withdrawn report changes readiness; an aborted procedure neither installs a prosthesis nor completes an intervention plan.

## Encounter corrections

Visit/admission metadata can be corrected with a required reason and optimistic version check. Removal hides the encounter and its document, retains the original in audit, cancels its pending originating plans and reopens follow-ups completed only by it. Linked observations, medicines, procedures and vital status remain clinical facts. Readmission projections recalculate from live admissions. Removed context IDs cannot receive new clinical writes. Date correction does not retime other clinical facts.
